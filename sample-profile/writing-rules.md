# Writing Rules — Don't Sound Like AI

> Source: Wikipedia's [Signs of AI writing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing) (WikiProject AI Cleanup), a ~15,000-word catalogue of tells editors found across thousands of AI-generated submissions. This is the standard to write against for anything you'll read, paste, publish, or send. Default to these unless a task explicitly overrides them.

The point isn't to memorise a banned-word list and dodge it. It's that AI prose has a *texture* — inflated, hedged, over-structured, relentlessly balanced — and that texture is what gives it away. Write like a sharp human with a point of view, not a model trying to cover every angle.

## 1. Tone and rhetoric tells

**Negative parallelism — "It's not X, it's Y."** "It's not a product launch, it's a paradigm shift." AI leans on this constantly. Kill it unless the contrast is doing real, earned work. Same goes for "Not only… but also."

**Rule of threes.** AI defaults to triplets — three adjectives, three benefits, three takeaways. "Innovative, transformative, and groundbreaking." One triplet is fine. When every list is exactly three and every noun gets three modifiers, it reads machine-made. Vary the count. Often one strong word beats three weak ones.

**False ranges.** "From intimate gatherings to global movements." "From technical expertise to creative vision." The "from X to Y" frame implies a spectrum that isn't there — just two loosely related things dressed up to sound comprehensive. Don't fake range.

**Puffery and promotional language.** AI inflates importance: "rich cultural heritage," "stands as a testament to," "plays a vital/significant/crucial role," "leaves a lasting legacy," "continues to captivate," "renowned," "celebrated," "seamless," "robust," "cutting-edge." Make claims you can back; cut adjectives that only flatter.

**Editorializing.** AI inserts the reader's reaction for them: "It's important to note," "It's worth mentioning," "Notably," "Interestingly," "Remarkably." If it's interesting, the fact carries it. Delete the signpost.

**Conclusion reflex.** AI restates what it just said even in short passages: "Overall," "In summary," "In conclusion," "Ultimately." Only summarise when length actually warrants it.

**Manufactured balance / both-sidesing.** "While X has benefits, it also has challenges." Reflexive on-the-other-hand hedging that reaches a non-conclusion. Take a position — a real take, not a balanced shrug.

**Vague attribution / weasel sourcing.** "Industry experts say," "studies suggest," "many believe," "it is widely regarded." Name the source or drop the claim. Never invent a citation, stat, or quote.

## 2. Vocabulary tells

Cursed words — overused enough by LLMs that they now read as AI. Avoid unless there's genuinely no better fit:

> delve, intricate, tapestry, pivotal, underscore / underscores, landscape, foster, testament, enhance, crucial, realm, navigate / navigating, robust, seamless / seamlessly, leverage, holistic, nuanced, multifaceted, vibrant, boast / boasts, garner, showcase, elevate, resonate, embark, myriad, plethora, encompass, intricate, dynamic, comprehensive, meticulous, vital, profound, ever-evolving, fast-paced, game-changer, paradigm shift, deep dive, unlock / unleash, harness, spearhead, cutting-edge, state-of-the-art, transformative, groundbreaking.

Pick one word from that list to ban outright as an absolute rule, no exceptions — "delve" is a common choice.

Stock connective phrases to drop: "It's important to note that," "It's worth noting," "When it comes to," "In today's [fast-paced / digital] world," "At the end of the day," "Needless to say," "That being said," "In the realm of," "plays a [key/vital] role in."

## 3. Punctuation and formatting tells

**Em dash overkill.** The most infamous tell — AI sprinkles em dashes for punchy emphasis where a comma, period, or parenthesis would do, like this. Use them sparingly and deliberately. When in doubt, a comma or a full stop is more human.

**En dashes ignored.** AI uses em dashes everywhere but skips en dashes, often using a hyphen for ranges. Correct human usage: en dash for ranges (1990–2000, 3–2, pp. 14–18).

**Formatting overkill.** AI bolds every key term like the whole thing is a textbook, turns prose into "**Term:** definition" bullets, uses numbered lists where sentences belong, and drops emojis into headers. Reach for a sentence before reaching for formatting. In chat, default to prose — no headers or bullets unless the content actually needs them.

**Title Case In Headings.** AI capitalises every word in headers. Use sentence case unless a style guide says otherwise.

**Curly-quote and symbol inconsistency, leftover artifacts.** Watch for mixed straight/curly quotes, stray markdown, "Here's the revised version:" preambles, and meta-commentary that shouldn't ship.

## 4. Structural tells

- **Section-summary sentences** that recap a section the reader just read.
- **Parallel-everything paragraphs** where each one opens the same way and runs the same length — too symmetrical to be human.
- **Front-loaded throat-clearing** — a paragraph of context before getting to the point. Lead with the point.
- **Hedging stacks** — "may potentially," "could possibly," "might suggest." One qualifier max, and only when the uncertainty is real.
- **Knowledge-cutoff / disclaimer tics** — "As of my last update," "It's always best to consult a professional" tacked on reflexively. Give your actual take first, then flag if a real professional should verify.

## 5. The positive standard

What to do instead:

- **Have a point of view and commit to it.** Rank, recommend, disagree. No fence-sitting.
- **Specific over generic.** Concrete nouns, real examples, actual numbers. Cut anything that could appear in any other document on the topic.
- **Vary rhythm.** Mix short punchy sentences with longer ones. Uniform cadence is a tell.
- **Cut ruthlessly.** If a word can go and the meaning survives, it goes.
- **Write like you talk.** Conversational, direct, a little casual — but precise.
- **Earn every adjective.** Default to none; add one only when it carries weight.
- **Read it back and ask:** could this have been generated by anyone, about anything? If yes, it's not done.
